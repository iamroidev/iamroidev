# Chevening Essay 2: Networking and Relationship Building (Max 500 Words)

**Applicant:** Richard Kwaku Opoku  
**Prompt:** Chevening is looking for individuals with strong networking skills, who will establish and maintain networks to bring about positive change. Outline how you currently establish and develop networks and how you will use the Chevening network in the future.

---

### Essay Draft: (~480 words)

Effective networking is rooted in mutual value, active collaboration, and the purposeful bridging of diverse communities. Rather than accumulating passive social connections, I establish relationships by contributing technical expertise, engaging in developer collectives, and connecting academic talent with industry opportunities.

At the University of Mines and Technology (UMaT), I recognized early that cybersecurity and software engineering are collective disciplines that require cross-pollination. To cultivate a culture of hands-on defense, I became an active contributor to the **UMaT Cybersecurity Club**. Within this network, I established technical study groups where we collaborate weekly on network reconnaissance using Nmap, simulated exploitation labs, and SQL injection vulnerability mitigation. By organizing collaborative challenge-solving sessions, I bridged friendships across junior and senior cohorts, creating an informal knowledge-sharing network where members share threat intelligence, open-source tools, and security research findings.

Externally, my engagement with **AmaliTech** and the global **One Million Coders (OMC)** initiative provided a broader forum to expand my professional circle. During the intensive AWS re/Start cloud program, I intentionally formed a cross-functional hackathon team with peers specializing in database design and frontend development. By managing team communication and establishing transparent Git workflows, our team engineered an end-to-end hospital queue management application that integrated AWS Cognito identity management and serverless Lambda functions. This relationship has persisted beyond the program; we continue to review each other’s code, share remote job leads, and exchange insights on cloud security certifications.

Furthermore, building open-source projects such as **Scholar** ([schorla.vercel.app](https://schorla.vercel.app)) and **InsightFlow** ([appinsightflow.vercel.app](https://appinsightflow.vercel.app)) expanded my network to international developer communities on GitHub and LinkedIn. Engaging directly with engineers across North America and Europe regarding NLP parsing and RAG pipeline optimization taught me how to navigate cross-cultural technical discussions and solicit critical architectural feedback.

The Chevening network represents an unparalleled global platform of innovators, policymakers, and industry pioneers. As a Chevening Scholar in the UK, I will actively engage with the National Cyber Security Centre (NCSC) student forums, UK Cyber Clusters, and British university incubators. I will connect with fellow scholars across disciplines—linking with public policy scholars to understand cyber legislation and with business scholars to explore tech commercialization models.

Upon returning to Ghana, I will mobilize these global relationships to establish collaborative cyber defense initiatives between UK and Ghanaian tech ecosystems. By acting as a liaison between the Chevening Alumni network, local universities, and West African fintech hubs, I will organize bilateral cybersecurity hackathons, facilitate guest lectures, and connect local engineering talent with global security mentorship. Chevening will provide the lifelong community through which I can cross-pollinate international security standards into West Africa’s digital economy.
