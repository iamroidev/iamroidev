# Germany CV Format Guide: Tabular Lebenslauf & Europass 🇩🇪📄

When applying to German universities (such as **Saarland University / CISPA** or **TU Darmstadt**) and the **DAAD Scholarship**, the standard format is the **Tabular CV (Tabellarischer Lebenslauf)** or the **Europass CV**.

German academic and scholarship reviewers expect a clean, structured, no-fluff layout with specific traditional elements.

---

## 🔑 Crucial Rules for a German CV

| Element | German Standard / Expectation |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Layout Structure** | **Tabular (Two columns):** Exact dates on the left (`MM/YYYY – MM/YYYY` or `MM/YYYY – Present`), institution and detailed achievements on the right. |
| **Chronology** | **Reverse Chronological:** Most recent education and experience first. |
| **Personal Details** | In Germany, unlike the US or UK, academic/DAAD CVs standardly include: Full Name, Date of Birth, Nationality/Citizenship, Location, Professional Email, Phone, LinkedIn, and GitHub. |
| **Photo (Bewerbungsfoto)** | A professional, friendly, high-resolution portrait in the top right corner is traditional and widely appreciated by German review committees. |
| **Language Skills** | Must be rated according to the **CEFR (Common European Framework of Reference)** scale: A1/A2 (Basic), B1/B2 (Independent), C1/C2 (Proficient/Mastery). E.g., *English: C2 (Native / Medium of Instruction)*. |
| **Length** | Typically **1 to 2 pages** max for a Master's applicant. |
| **Closing Convention** | German CVs traditionally end with the **Location, Date, and your Signature** (e.g., *Tarkwa, Ghana, 15 October 2026 — [Signature]*). |
